01 — Discover

Competitive Analysis & Audience Research

Before any design work started, the process opened with three research tracks: a direct audit of the client's current live site and brand identity, a competitive review of how top-producing luxury brokers win on Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and TikTok, and an audience-research pass to define exactly who the refreshed brand and site have to convert.

AI in this stageUsed to accelerate synthesis — cross-referencing dozens of competitor accounts and industry reports into the platform findings below in hours rather than days, so more time went to interpreting the findings, not just collecting them.

Branding & Positioning

Legacy name, understated track record

The live site still uses the full name "Glice Parente" rather than the simplified "Glice Lima" identity the refresh is moving toward. Its bio leads with 12 years of experience and "Top Producer for the last seven years" — short of the 15-year, tri-county positioning the refresh is built on. No mention of commercial expertise or the 360° network.

Geographic Coverage

Miami-only, with one typo'd exception

14 listings total across Home and Properties, almost entirely in Miami-Dade (Coral Gables, Miami Beach, Brickell). The one Palm Beach-area listing has "Delray" misspelled "Delrey" in its own URL and title. No Broward presence, no county or neighborhood navigation exists.

Language & Accessibility

Bilingual, not trilingual

The header offers English and Portuguese only (via TranslatePress). There is no Spanish toggle — a direct gap against the plan's EN/PT/ES positioning built specifically to reach Latin American buyers from Brazil, Colombia and Argentina.

Lead Generation

Two shallow conversion paths

The only conversions on the site are a WhatsApp link and a generic name/email/message contact form. There's no market-report opt-in, no CRM segmentation by county or language, and no scheduling tool.

Listings & Technology

No live inventory

Properties are static WordPress posts linked via "Read More" — there is no MLS/IDX feed, no filtering by price, beds, baths or county, and listings must be manually rebuilt as they sell.

Trust & Social Proof

Real testimonials, no amplification

Five genuine client testimonials are a real asset worth carrying forward — but there are no press mentions, no video testimonials, and no sales-volume stats anywhere on the site.

Every finding above lines up directly with the gaps defined in the next stage: this isn't a site that simply needs a fresh coat of paint — it's structurally unable to carry the refreshed Glice Lima identity, or her 15 year track record, tri-county coverage, trilingual reach, and 360° service network the plan is built around.

Boca Raton-based Matouk makes viewers feel they're touring alongside him. Serhant trains agents to sound natural, not salesy; Yilmazer built $50–100K/month in ad revenue on a laid-back, informational tone.

Modern Design & Style · Nest Seekers

An Open Lane

Most luxury residential agents underinvest here. It's the highest-credibility channel for relocating executives and referral partners — a direct match for the commercial background and 360° network being built out.

Industry commentary, commercial & luxury RE

Undervalued Reach

Distribution favors relevance over follower count. Heider (3.7M followers) teases rather than narrates; completion-rate bar rose to ~70% in 2026. No competitor reviewed offers trilingual content — an unclaimed opportunity here.

Placester · Noseberry Digitals (Coldwell Banker data)

Headline stat driving the strategy: 73% of affluent buyers say social media has influenced a specific dream-home purchase, with Instagram, YouTube and TikTok at near parity (44–45% each) — luxury buyers are already platform-agnostic, so the site and content system had to be too.

02 — Define

Identifying the Gap

Research made the core problem clear: the public identity and digital presence hadn't caught up to the substance of the business, marketing materials were split across two names mid-transition, and the strongest competitors were winning on repeatable systems rather than one-off content.

AI in this stageUsed as a sounding board to pressure-test the problem statement against the raw audit evidence — flagging where a claim needed a citation before it became a plan.

"Glice Lima Parente has spent 15 years building tri-county reach, trilingual fluency and a genuine 360° service network — but she's marketing under a name and a site that don't yet reflect any of it. A slight rebrand to 'Glice Lima' needs a digital presence built to match, not just a new logo."

Gap 01

Marketing materials are currently split across two names — the live site says "Glice Parente," while the new logo, wireframe and homepage concepts already use "Glice Lima" — an inconsistent identity mid-transition.

Audit evidence: live site branding, meta tags and social handles all still read "Glice Parente."

Gap 02

A single-platform, Instagram-only footprint doesn't match how platform-agnostic luxury buyers actually shop across Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and TikTok.

Audit evidence: current site links only Instagram, Facebook & LinkedIn — no YouTube or TikTok presence.

Gap 03

No site structure exists to carry three counties, three languages, and eleven+ neighborhoods without duplicating effort or diluting local SEO.

Audit evidence: 14 listings, almost all Miami-Dade; English/Portuguese only, no Spanish; zero county or neighborhood navigation.

Gap 04

Top local competitors win with named, repeatable formats and status-marking systems — this site and content need the same institutionalized structure, not isolated assets.

Audit evidence: static WordPress posts with no IDX feed, no market-report capture, no repeatable content system in place.

03 — Plan

Building the Brand Refresh & Marketing Plan

This is a slight rebrand, not a reinvention: the name simplifies and the visual identity refreshes, but the license, the relationships and the 15-year track record carry forward unchanged. The gap analysis became a plan built on four pillars, translated directly into the site's core structure so every page has a clear job.

AI in this stageUsed to draft and compare multiple information-architecture options against the four pillars before committing to the site map below — narrowing the field faster so more review time went to stakeholder judgment calls.

06 — Validate

UX Research Plan — Testing the Recommended Design

Before development starts, the recommended homepage direction needs to be validated with real buyers against the same personas that shaped the plan — not just reviewed internally.

AI in this stageUsed to draft the first pass of research questions and task scripts against the five personas, then refined by hand before fielding — AI proposes, research craft disposes.
Research Questions
01Do repeat and referral clients who know her as "Glice Parente" still recognize and trust "Glice Lima" as the same person?
02Can visitors identify the differentiators — 15 years, trilingual, 360° network, tri-county coverage — within 10 seconds of landing?
03Can each target persona complete core conversion tasks (find a listing, request the market report, make contact) unassisted?
04Does the county/neighborhood navigation model make sense to buyers unfamiliar with South Florida geography?
05Is the EN/PT/ES language selector noticed and used appropriately by non-English-dominant participants?
Round 1 · Unmoderated

Concept Preference Test

  • 5-second first-impression test across Concepts V1 / V2 / V3
  • First-click test toward "View Listings" and "Contact"
  • Measures: clarity of value prop, perceived luxury/trust, click accuracy
n = 15–20 · Maze / UsabilityHub · 48-hour field window
Round 2 · Moderated

Task-Based Usability Testing

  • Find a home for sale in Delray Beach under $3M
  • Locate the professional network / find a recommended mortgage broker
  • Switch the site to Portuguese or Spanish
  • Sign up for the quarterly market report
  • Ask a question about a commercial property
n = 8, mapped to the 5 buyer personas · 60-min sessions, Zoom + Lookback
