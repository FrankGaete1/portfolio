A Brand Refresh, Backed by Research — Glice Parente to Glice Lima

Glice Lima Parente has always marketed under her full name. This project is a slight rebrand — simplifying her public identity to "Glice Lima" — paired with a digital marketing plan built to finally match her 15-year, tri-county track record. Here's how it moved from a brand and site audit through wireframes, high-fidelity homepage concepts, a clickable prototype, and a plan to validate the recommended design with real buyers.

01 — Discover

Competitive Analysis & Audience Research

Before any design work started, the process opened with three research tracks: a direct audit of Glice's current live site and brand identity, a competitive review of how top-producing luxury brokers win on Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and TikTok, and an audience-research pass to define exactly who the refreshed brand and site have to convert.

Current State
Site & Brand Audit — glicefloridarealtor.com

The starting point for discovery was Glice's own live site and current brand identity. She markets under her full name, Glice Parente — matching her legal name, Glice Lima Parente. The refresh underway simplifies that public identity to "Glice Lima," dropping the surname rather than changing who she is. Auditing the live site directly — rather than working from assumptions — turned up the exact evidence behind the gaps defined in the next stage.

Branding & Positioning

Legacy name, understated track record

The live site still uses the full name "Glice Parente" rather than the simplified "Glice Lima" identity the refresh is moving toward. Its bio leads with 12 years of experience and "Top Producer for the last seven years" — short of the 15-year, tri-county positioning the refresh is built on. No mention of commercial expertise or the 360° network.

Geographic Coverage

Miami-only, with one typo'd exception

14 listings total across Home and Properties, almost entirely in Miami-Dade (Coral Gables, Miami Beach, Brickell). The one Palm Beach-area listing has "Delray" misspelled "Delrey" in its own URL and title. No Broward presence, no county or neighborhood navigation exists.

Language & Accessibility

Bilingual, not trilingual

The header offers English and Portuguese only (via TranslatePress). There is no Spanish toggle — a direct gap against the plan's EN/PT/ES positioning built specifically to reach Latin American buyers from Brazil, Colombia and Argentina.

Lead Generation

Two shallow conversion paths

The only conversions on the site are a WhatsApp link and a generic name/email/message contact form. There's no market-report opt-in, no CRM segmentation by county or language, and no scheduling tool.

Listings & Technology

No live inventory

Properties are static WordPress posts linked via "Read More" — there is no MLS/IDX feed, no filtering by price, beds, baths or county, and listings must be manually rebuilt as they sell.

Trust & Social Proof

Real testimonials, no amplification

Five genuine client testimonials (Studart, Taillet, Chandrashekhar, Lima, Fainshmidt) are a real asset worth carrying forward — but there are no press mentions, no video testimonials, and no sales-volume stats anywhere on the site.

Every finding above lines up directly with the gaps defined in the next stage: this isn't a site that simply needs a fresh coat of paint — it's structurally unable to carry the refreshed Glice Lima identity, or her 15 years of track record, tri-county coverage, trilingual reach, and 360° service network the plan is built around.

02 — Define

Identifying the Gap

Research made the core problem clear: Glice's public identity and digital presence hadn't caught up to the substance of her business, her marketing materials were split across two names mid-transition, and her strongest competitors were winning on repeatable systems rather than one-off content.

"Glice Lima Parente has spent 15 years building tri-county reach, trilingual fluency and a genuine 360° service network — but she's marketing under a name and a site that don't yet reflect any of it. A slight rebrand to 'Glice Lima' needs a digital presence built to match, not just a new logo."

Gap 01

Marketing materials are currently split across two names — the live site says "Glice Parente," while the new logo, wireframe and homepage concepts already use "Glice Lima" — an inconsistent identity mid-transition.

Audit evidence: live site branding, meta tags and social handles all still read "Glice Parente."

Gap 02

A single-platform, Instagram-only footprint doesn't match how platform-agnostic luxury buyers actually shop across Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and TikTok.

Audit evidence: current site links only Instagram, Facebook & LinkedIn — no YouTube or TikTok presence.

Gap 03

No site structure exists to carry three counties, three languages, and eleven+ neighborhoods without duplicating effort or diluting local SEO.

Audit evidence: 14 listings, almost all Miami-Dade; English/Portuguese only, no Spanish; zero county or neighborhood navigation.

Gap 04

Top local competitors win with named, repeatable formats and status-marking systems — Glice's site and content need the same institutionalized structure, not isolated assets.

Audit evidence: static WordPress posts with no IDX feed, no market-report capture, no repeatable content system in place.

03 — Plan

Building the Brand Refresh & Marketing Plan

This is a slight rebrand, not a reinvention: the name simplifies and the visual identity refreshes, but the license, the relationships and the 15-year track record carry forward unchanged. The gap analysis became a plan built on four pillars, translated directly into the site's core structure so every page has a clear job.

What's Changing
  • Public name: Glice Parente → Glice Lima
  • Visual identity refreshed to the navy & gold skyline mark
  • Site rebuilt around the four pillars below, with a real tri-county, trilingual structure
  • Domain, social handles and collateral consolidated under one consistent name
What's Staying the Same
  • Glice Lima Parente remains her legal and licensed name
  • 15 years of experience, client relationships and referral network carry forward as-is
  • Existing testimonials and past sales history remain valid and reusable
  • Brokerage license, affiliations and credentials are unaffected

05 — Design & Prototype

High-Fidelity Concepts for Review

Once the structure was validated, the wireframe was translated into three high-fidelity homepage directions — same content and brand system, each testing a different layout and a different use of the brand's navy as a background color to highlight key sections.

Concept V1 — Full-Bleed Navy Hero

Navy anchors the hero and the "Explore by County" band; an editorial, asymmetric listings grid and a gold market-report band close the page.

Concept V2 — Split-Screen, Navy Bookend

An asymmetric split hero pairs a solid navy panel with property photography; navy returns for the services band and the closing CTA, framing the page top and bottom.

Concept V3 — Color-Blocked, Navy-Dominant

A solid navy header and light, airy hero set up bold navy bands for county coverage and a testimonial spotlight — the most navy-forward of the three.

Clickable Prototype

From Static Screens to a Click-Through Flow

The same wireframe file doubles as the clickable prototype used in stakeholder review: every card, nav link and CTA is wired to its destination screen, so reviewers can click through the real navigation logic rather than looking at flat images.

06 — Validate

UX Research Plan — Testing the Recommended Design

Before development starts, the recommended homepage direction needs to be validated with real buyers against the same personas that shaped the plan — not just reviewed internally.

Research Questions
01Do repeat and referral clients who know her as "Glice Parente" still recognize and trust "Glice Lima" as the same person?
02Can visitors identify Glice's differentiators — 15 years, trilingual, 360° network, tri-county coverage — within 10 seconds of landing?
03Can each target persona complete core conversion tasks (find a listing, request the market report, contact Glice) unassisted?
04Does the county/neighborhood navigation model make sense to buyers unfamiliar with South Florida geography?
05Is the EN/PT/ES language selector noticed and used appropriately by non-English-dominant participants?
Round 1 · Unmoderated

Concept Preference Test

  • 5-second first-impression test across Concepts V1 / V2 / V3
  • First-click test toward "View Listings" and "Contact Glice"
  • Measures: clarity of value prop, perceived luxury/trust, click accuracy
n = 15–20 · Maze / UsabilityHub · 48-hour field window
Round 2 · Moderated

Task-Based Usability Testing

  • Find a home for sale in Delray Beach under $3M
  • Locate the professional network / find a recommended mortgage broker
  • Switch the site to Portuguese or Spanish
  • Sign up for the quarterly market report
  • Contact Glice about a commercial property
n = 8, mapped to the 5 buyer personas · 60-min sessions, Zoom + Lookback
